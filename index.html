About

Robert Smith is a health economist and data scientist based in Sheffield, UK. His work applies methods from data science, including R, Shiny, automation and AI, to make health economic models more transparent, reproducible and useful for decision makers.

He is Chief Scientific Officer at Initiate, where he leads scientific strategy and the group's health economics, data science and AI capabilities. In 2019 he co-founded Dark Peak Analytics with Dr Paul Schneider, and grew it into a team of health economists, statisticians and software developers before it joined Initiate in 2026. Dark Peak has delivered more than 100 projects for over 50 clients, including the World Health Organization and many of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies. It also builds software, including a Structured Expert Elicitation Platform used to support submissions to NICE and other HTA bodies, early economic modelling and survival analysis platforms, and geospatial access models. He developed and maintains assertHE, an R package on CRAN for quality assurance of health economic models, and leads Dark Peak's online short courses in R for health economics, which have enrolled more than 1,000 participants worldwide.

Robert is co-director of the R for HTA consortium, which brings together industry, HTA bodies and academics to build shared R tools and methods for health technology assessment, with a focus on Living HTA and transparent, reproducible models. From 2018 to 2024 he was an expert advisor to the World Health Organization's Health Economic Assessment Tool (HEAT) for walking and cycling.

During the COVID-19 pandemic he was Lead Epidemiologist and Statistical Programmer at the Joint Biosecurity Centre, part of the UK Health Security Agency. There he built an infectious disease model with an interactive interface that was used across government, including by the Department of Health and Social Care, Number 10, the Cabinet Office and the Office for Budget Responsibility. Before that he was Principal Health Economist at Lumanity (formerly BresMed), where he led internal R training and built R Shiny models for pharmaceutical clients.

He holds a PhD in Public Health, Economics and Decision Science from SCHARR, funded through the Wellcome Trust doctoral programme, on improving the validity and usability of decision models, with a focus on physical activity and parkrun. He also holds a BA and an MSc in Economics from the University of Nottingham. His research has been published in journals including The Lancet Global Health, PharmacoEconomics, Epidemics, Medical Decision Making, Health Economics and Wellcome Open Research.
